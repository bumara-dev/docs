Bumara gives each regulator its own workspace. They all look and work the same way, so learning
one teaches you the rest.

## The regulators covered

Two further bodies are covered by the [compliance health check](/start/compliance-health-check)
and, on higher plans, by full workspaces: the **Workers' Compensation Fund** and your
**local council** for trading licences and business levies.

## What connecting a regulator does

Connecting is not just recording a number. It is what makes Bumara start working for you.

<Steps>
  <Step title="You enter your registration details">
    Your employer number or taxpayer identifier with that body, and any scheme selections — for
    ZRA, which tax types you are registered for.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Bumara works out your obligations">
    It compares your entity type and your selections against what that regulator requires, and
    creates the obligations that apply to you.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Filings are generated with due dates">
    Each obligation produces a filing for the current period, with a due date calculated using the
    regulator's own rule.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Checklists appear">
    Each filing arrives with its tasks and its document requirements, so you know what to do.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Services become available">
    The service catalogue for that regulator opens up, filtered to what your entity type and plan
    allow.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Note>
  Only tick the tax types and schemes you are genuinely registered for. Ticking PAYE when you have
  no employees creates a monthly obligation you will spend a year filing nil returns against.
</Note>

## The workspace layout

Every regulator workspace has the same pages:

| Page | What is there |
| --- | --- |
| **Overview** | Active obligations, open filings, next due date, task progress |
| **Obligations** | The recurring requirements for this regulator |
| **Filings** | One entry per period |
| **Service requests** | One-off jobs, and the catalogue you can request from |
| **Tasks** | Everything outstanding for this regulator, in one list |
| **Documents** | Everything filed with or received from this regulator |
| **Timeline** | Full activity history |
| **Authorisation** | Your named representative, where the regulator requires one |

## What Bumara does on your behalf

| Bumara does | You do |
| --- | --- |
| Works out which obligations apply to you | Confirm they look right |
| Calculates due dates | Meet them |
| Generates the checklist for each filing | Work through it |
| Pre-fills figures from your payroll and invoicing | Review and confirm them |
| Lodges the submission with the regulator | Request the submission |
| Records the acknowledgement in your vault | Nothing |
| Warns you before deadlines and expiries | Act on the warnings |
| Reviews your standing at each regulator on request | Provide access and answer queries |

Bumara does not decide what your tax position should be, and it does not file anything you have
not asked it to file.

## Adding a regulator later

You can connect a new regulator at any time. Obligations are created from the moment you connect —
Bumara does not retroactively generate filings for periods that have already passed.

If you have missed periods with a regulator you are only now connecting, run a
[compliance health check](/start/compliance-health-check) so the historical position is established
properly, with the correct penalties and interest, rather than guessed at.

## Disconnecting a regulator

If you cease to be registered with a regulator — you deregister for a tax type, or the business
changes form — tell Bumara so the obligations stop being created.

Existing filings are kept. Your history with that regulator does not disappear, because you may
need to evidence it later.

<Warning>
  Disconnecting a regulator does not deregister you with that regulator. That is a separate process
  you must complete with them directly, usually as a service request. Stopping the reminders is not
  the same as stopping the obligation.
</Warning>
